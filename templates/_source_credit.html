{# The credit Wiktionary's licence asks for (#390). Its definitions are CC BY-SA 4.0, which permits exactly what this app does with them -- copy one onto a card, verbatim -- on the condition that the source is named, the entry reachable and the licence linked. So this is not decoration: it is the condition of the text being here at all, and it has to travel with the text to every surface that shows one. That is why it is a partial rather than three copies of a paragraph. `card.explanation_source` is the whole test. Oxford and Reverso need no credit, an explanation nobody recorded a source for gets none, and a card whose text a learner has since edited had its source cleared on the way in (`update_flashcard`) -- because the sentence is then theirs, not Wiktionary's, and crediting it to Wiktionary would be the misattribution the licence actually cares about. Needs `card.word` as well: the entry link is derived from it rather than stored, so a template including this with a different variable name has to say `{% with card=r %}`. **Short because the deck face is 183px wide on a phone**, measured. The sentence this started as ("Definition from Wiktionary, ...") needed 224px and wrapped to two lines, which pushed the text out of a flip card whose height is fixed. This wording is 167px and stays on one line. It still does the three things the licence asks -- names the source, links the entry, names and links the licence -- and it sits directly under the definition it credits, which is what supplies the word the wording dropped. #} {% if card.explanation_source == 'wiktionary' %}
